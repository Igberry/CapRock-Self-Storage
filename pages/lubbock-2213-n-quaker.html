City Information

About Lubbock

Lubbock is the largest city on the South Plains of West Texas and the seat of Lubbock County. It is known as the Hub City because it functions as the commercial, medical and educational centre for a wide stretch of surrounding farmland, pulling in far more people than its own population of roughly a quarter of a million would suggest.

Getting around

Interstate 27 runs north to south through the city, Loop 289 rings it, and US 84, the Marsha Sharp Freeway, carries traffic out to the west.

Work and study

Texas Tech University and the Texas Tech University Health Sciences Center anchor both the local economy and the city's character, and the student population turns over enough each year to keep storage in steady demand. Lubbock Christian University and a Lubbock campus of South Plains College add to that. On the healthcare side, Covenant Health and University Medical Center are among the largest employers in the region.

Outside the campuses and hospitals, agriculture still defines the surrounding South Plains, where cotton is the dominant crop. Families moving for a job at the university, the hospitals or in agribusiness make up a good share of the people who need a unit for a few months.

Schools and neighbourhoods

Lubbock is served by several districts, principally Lubbock ISD, along with Frenship ISD to the west and Lubbock-Cooper ISD to the south. Households moving between these districts, or in and out of the neighbourhoods around Texas Tech, are a common reason for a short-term unit while a sale or a lease lines up.

Things to do

The Buddy Holly Center and the Buddy Holly Hall of Performing Arts and Sciences mark the city's best-known musical export. Texas Tech is home to the National Ranching Heritage Center, and the Lubbock Lake Landmark preserves an archaeological site on the northern edge of town. The Depot District carries much of the city's live music and nightlife, and Lubbock sits at the centre of the Texas High Plains, which grows a large share of the state's wine grapes.

Military

Reese Air Force Base, west of the city, closed in 1997, and the site now operates as Reese Technology Center, a research and business park. There is no longer an active installation in Lubbock itself, though service members relocating through the region still store here.
